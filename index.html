How we work

Four steps, no surprises

  1. 01

    Start with a 15-minute conversation

    We’ll ask about your current process, what’s frustrating or expensive about it, and what you’d like to improve. We’ll also decide whether we’re the right people to help.

    If the problem doesn’t justify building anything, we’ll tell you.

  2. 02

    Prove the idea quickly

    If we see a practical solution, we’ll aim to show you a small proof of concept within 24–48 hours, where the problem and scope allow. It could be:

    • A clickable prototype
    • A screen-sharing demo
    • A short recorded walkthrough
    • A small technical proof

    It shows feasibility and direction, not a finished product. For complex problems, we’ll agree a realistic next step instead.

  3. 03

    Agree on the plan, then build

    Before implementation begins, we align on scope, deliverables, timeline, costs, and how we’ll judge success. You’ll know what you’re paying for and what the finished work should accomplish.

  4. 04

    Stay in the loop

    You won’t have to chase us for updates. At the start, we agree a rhythm that suits the work: short daily updates, weekly calls, milestone demos, or something else.

    Questions, concerns and changes get discussed openly, not left until the deadline.

FAQ

Common questions

Do we need to use AI?

No. We choose an approach based on the problem. A simple integration or workflow improvement may be better than an AI solution.

What happens during the first call?

We understand the current process, the problem, the expected outcome, and whether there is a sensible next step.

What do you mean by a 24–48-hour demonstration?

A focused proof of concept or walkthrough, when appropriate, to illustrate the proposed direction. It is not a promise of a finished production system within that timeframe.

How often will we communicate?

We agree on the appropriate update and meeting schedule at the start of a project and keep you informed throughout delivery.

What happens if you can’t help?

We’ll be straightforward about the limitations and won’t recommend a project simply to make a sale.
